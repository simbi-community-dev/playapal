# Safety, Medical, and Rangers — Black Rock City 2026

## Medical stations [2026-confirmed]
- Locations: 3:00 & C, 9:00 & C, and the main station at 5:15 & Esplanade. Look for the lighted red cross above each station.
- The 5:15 & Esplanade site is the city's main medical center: the Emergency Services Department (ESD) station and the Rampart Emergency Care Center are co-located there. Plaza stations handle walk-in first aid; serious cases go to the main station, and critical patients are flown/driven to Reno hospitals (off-playa transport is at the patient's expense). [2026-confirmed: Survival Guide "On-Playa Resources", "Health", and "Consent" pages all say 5:15 & Esplanade; the "Getting Help" page's "Esplanade near 6:30" is Ranger HQ's address, not medical. Transport/cost detail: evergreen, UNVERIFIED for 2026]
- Playa foot: cracked, burning skin on your feet from the alkaline dust — very common. Soak 20 minutes a day in a 4:1 water-to-vinegar mix, then lotion and socks; closed shoes and socks prevent it. Treat yourself or your campmates first (camp first-aid kit); if it is worse than you can manage, go to a medical station. [2026-confirmed: Survival Guide "Health"]
- Medical help can also be summoned through any Ranger, staff member with a radio, or BRC 911 (see Radio file).

## Black Rock Rangers [2026-confirmed]
- Veteran Burners who volunteer to help participants resolve conflicts and find solutions. They are not police.
- Recognize them by khaki attire with the Burning Man logo and white hats with the Ranger logo.
- They mediate disagreements, locate lost children, address safety concerns, direct people to resources, and can radio for law enforcement, medical, mental health, fire, or environmental teams.
- Available 24/7. Ranger HQ: Esplanade & 6:30. Ranger outposts: 3:00 and 9:00 plazas. Pairs patrol the whole city on foot/bike.
- High Rock Security: contract security team in black "High Rock Security" shirts supporting the Rangers; they prefer de-escalation and escalate serious issues to law enforcement.

## Emergencies [2026-confirmed]
- Flag down a Ranger, law enforcement, or any event staff/volunteer with a radio; or go to a Ranger outpost or medical station.
- BRC 911 Dispatch by personal radio: 451.9000 MHz, CTCSS/PL tone 91.5.
- Urgent messages from the outside world: email 911@burningman.org (delivered by Rangers); check Playa Info in Center Camp for incoming messages.
- Before arriving, make sure your people at home know: your full legal name and playa name, camp name/location, vehicle description and plate, and unique identifying features — that is what searchers use.

## Lost & Found [2026-confirmed]
- Location: Playa Info at Esplanade & 5:45. Database searchable 24/7; item pickup 9 am–6 pm.
- Label everything with your name to improve recovery odds. Lost & Found does not accept bicycles.
- After the event, lost & found moves online/to Reno via Playa Info's published process. [evergreen, UNVERIFIED 2026 details]

## Lost child protocol [evergreen — sourced from BMorg journal and Ranger practice]
- If your child is missing: go to the nearest Ranger immediately (Ranger HQ at 6:30 & Esplanade, outposts at 3:00 and 9:00 plazas, or any patrolling pair).
- Rangers initiate a Lost Child protocol: an all-hands alert to law enforcement and infrastructure staff, citizen alerts on BMIR, and the Gate closes to outbound traffic (including airport departures) until the child is found — usually within minutes.
- Teach children: if separated, find a Ranger (khaki, white hat) or any staff member with a radio, and stay put in a lit, public place.
- F.U.N. (Family Unification Network) program: register children at Ranger HQ (Esplanade & 6:30); each child gets a wristband with a unique serial number linked to guardians and camp location, so any Ranger can arrange a fast reunion.
- Kids 12 and under attend free but require a Kid's Ticket. [2026-confirmed]

## Whiteouts / dust storms [2026-confirmed protocol]
- Winds over 75 mph can drop visibility to zero within seconds.
- On foot or bike: stop, seek shelter if immediately available, otherwise sit down with your back to the wind and cover your face; put on your goggles and dust mask (carry them always); wait it out — whiteouts usually pass in minutes.
- Driving in a whiteout: stop the vehicle and wait; do not creep forward blind. [evergreen]

## Trip hazards, rebar, night visibility
- Rebar and tent stakes: hammer rebar fully into the ground where possible; cover any exposed stake ends with tennis balls, plastic bottles, or padding; candy-cane (bent-top) rebar reduces snag risk. Tripping on rebar and guy-lines is a classic playa injury. Mark guy-lines and stakes with lights or bright tape at night. [evergreen — sourced from burningman.org Rebar Safety page and community guides]
- Night visibility: the city is unlit and full of bikes and art cars. Wear lights after dark — EL wire, LED strings, headlamps, bike head- and tail-lights. A person or bike moving in the dark with no lights is called a "darkwad" and is a genuine collision hazard. Glow sticks are considered insufficient and become trash. [evergreen; bike-light rules 2026-confirmed]
- Handheld lasers are banned in BRC; mounted lasers require licensing/compliance. Drones are forbidden without prior registration and pre-flight tagging by the Safety Team. [2026-confirmed]
- Secure valuables, especially on Burn night (locking them in your car is suggested); photograph serial numbers in case a police report is needed. [2026-confirmed]
- Climbing on art: only where the artist explicitly allows it; structures are engineered for wind/load, not always for climbers. [evergreen]
