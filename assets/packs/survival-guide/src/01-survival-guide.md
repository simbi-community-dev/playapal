# Survival Basics — Burning Man 2026 (from the official Survival Guide, survival.burningman.org)

Black Rock City (BRC) is a temporary city built in Nevada's remote Black Rock Desert for Burning Man. Every participant is responsible for their own survival, safety, comfort, and well-being, and for Leaving No Trace. There is almost nothing for sale in the city (see Practical FAQs file): you must bring everything you need. [2026-confirmed]

## Water and hydration [2026-confirmed]
- Bring 1.5 gallons of water per person per day, covering drinking, washing, and cleaning.
- Water must be potable, from sources approved by the Nevada State Health Division.
- Always carry a full reusable water bottle when leaving camp.
- Dehydration symptoms: headaches, stomach cramps, constipation, flu-like symptoms, mood swings.
- Alcohol, caffeine, and drugs all increase dehydration risk. Electrolyte replacement helps alongside plain water.

## Heat and sun [2026-confirmed]
- Daytime temperatures routinely exceed 100°F (38°C). Overnight lows can reach the 40s°F (single digits °C).
- Elevation is nearly 4,000 feet, so skin burns faster and more severely than at sea level.
- Apply sunscreen every morning and reapply as needed; bring lip balm and lotion.
- Build shade structures for camp; rest during the hottest part of the day.
- Wide-brim hat recommended, with a chinstrap for wind.

## Dust and wind [2026-confirmed]
- Desert winds can exceed 75 mph and cause instant whiteouts (zero-visibility dust storms).
- Always carry a dust mask and goggles; goggles protect eyes during dust storms.
- If caught in a whiteout: seek shelter, sit down with your back to the wind, cover your face, and wait it out.

## Food [2026-confirmed]
- Bring all the food and beverages you will need for your whole stay.
- A cooking stove is optional, needed only if you plan to heat food or liquid.
- Ice is the only food-preservation item sold in the city (see Practical FAQs file).

## What to bring (Survival Guide packing list) [2026-confirmed]
- Good tent or shelter; warm sleeping bag and bedding; warm clothing; rain gear; sturdy footwear.
- 1.5 gal water/person/day; all food; reusable water bottle and cup.
- Dust mask and goggles; sunscreen; wide-brim hat.
- Extensive first-aid kit; hand sanitizer (60%+ alcohol); prescriptions in original containers; contact lens supplies.
- Flashlights/headlamps and spare batteries; bike lights and personal lights for night visibility (glow sticks are insufficient and become trash).
- Durable materials that will not break apart or scatter; aluminum cans for beverages (preferred over glass or plastic).
- A MOOP bag to carry everywhere; a pocket ashtray (e.g. mint tin) if you smoke.
- Tarps for kitchen/build/work areas; proper tie-downs for camp items and vehicle loads.
- Handled jug for daily MOOP collection around camp.
- Greywater containment system — no greywater may touch the playa.
- Secondary containment bins (e.g. kiddie pools) for any fuel containers.

## What to leave at home (MOOP-prone items) [2026-confirmed]
- Glitter, feathers, rhinestones, sequins, artificial grass.
- Wigs that shed, styrofoam coolers, plastic bags, loose paper, string, plastic cups.
- Hay, straw, gravel; plastic drink containers when avoidable.
- Everything you bring can become Matter Out of Place (MOOP) and must go home with you.

## Culture basics [evergreen]
- Burning Man is guided by the 10 Principles (see Ten Principles file).
- There is no money economy inside the city: no vending, no sponsorships, no advertising. Gifting is unconditional and expects nothing in return; Burning Man is not a barter economy.
- The Survival Guide's sections cover Culture, Transportation, City Infrastructure, Getting Around BRC, Survival/Health/Safety, Rules & Regulations, Kids & Families, Law Enforcement, and Leaving No Trace. A PDF version of the guide exists for offline reading.
