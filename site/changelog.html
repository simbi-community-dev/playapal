The road to the playa

Playa Pal was built in twelve days before Burning Man 2026 — the first commit landed Aug 13 and 0.8.0 shipped Aug 24. Each tenth of a version is an era: a thing the app couldn't do the day before. 1.0 is earned when Black Rock City itself has field-tested it.

0.8.5 · The talk button proves it

Aug 26 · one screenshot and one system log, half the answer each

  • The iPhone talk button proves the mic is alive — and heals it when it isn't: 0.8.4 cured how the audio engine is born; the holdout was how it ages. A video call rebuilds the phone's shared audio session behind the walkie's back, and the engine the walkie keeps warm survives that looking healthy while its microphone half is quietly dead — so every press read "no-audio" even though a fresh engine worked perfectly. Now the walkie checks the mic's pulse on every reuse (a dead one has a readable tell), rebuilds the engine the moment a call ends, and — if a press still hears nothing for one second — swaps in a fresh engine before ever bothering you.

0.8.4 · The phones that make their own network

Aug 26 · the overnight train: one night, eleven landings

  • One Android makes the Wi-Fi: video calls need a shared Wi-Fi and the playa doesn't have one — so the pod does. Flip on Camp hotspot and one Android hosts a no-internet Wi-Fi the others join by pointing a camera at the code it shows (iPhones offer to join straight from the stock Camera app). Fourteen ways hosting can fail, fourteen honest sentences.
  • Two iPhones link directly (new — being field-tested): pair once with a six-digit code on both screens, then talk at full quality with no Wi-Fi at all, the same no-network trick two Androids already do. Cross-platform with zero Wi-Fi stays lo-fi voice and messages — that half waits on Apple.
  • The talk button hears again: the iPhone mic bug came apart into three root causes — an audio engine born without its input half, a session mode whose voice processor delivered nothing, and a stray double-catch that swallowed every error. All three fixed at the root. And a project-file collision that had silently left one iOS module pair out of every build to date is found, fixed, and pinned.
  • Curiosity gets a tap: the tiny-print paragraphs on every screen moved behind a calm circled ? — one tap, full explanation, and the screen keeps its ink for what changes. Status lines stay put. Settings grew a text size dial — four steps, instant, everywhere.
  • Voice notes grow up: a graze can't mint an empty voicemail (a note needs a held second, and a shorter press says so), and received notes became a full-width play control with a bright playing state — hittable with gloves on.
  • The radio keeps its cool: a reviewer pass closed four race conditions in the night's radio surgery before they could bite, including a crash reachable from network input.

0.8.3 · The link that holds in the dust

Aug 26 · one night of three-phone field testing, distilled

  • The walkie stops flapping: with no Wi-Fi anywhere — the playa's normal — the phone-to-phone link used to come up, go deaf, and re-dial in an endless 30-second loop, and a name on the channel could look fine while nothing got through. Two root causes found in one night of two-sided logs and fixed: a re-dialed phone now re-learns where its podmate answers from instead of latching the first address forever, and an answering phone abandons a link the system quietly killed instead of staying bound to its corpse. Measured before and after: the loop is gone — one blip in five minutes, healed in two seconds.
  • The channel list stops lying: a podmate whose link went quiet now reads (quiet) — not (lo-fi), which promised live audio over a link that had none — with one sentence under the list saying your voice may not be reaching them while messages still go. And when names drop off the channel and come back as links flap, the app says so: that's the dust, not a fault.
  • Call endings tell the truth: "No answer" used to cover the case where the invite never left your phone. That's now its own ending — "Couldn't get through — their link went quiet" — and when that person still carries lo-fi voice, it says the working alternative out loud: hold the talk button.
  • Why some pairs are lo-fi: the walkie hint now leads with what to expect — some pairs of phones link at full quality, some always come through rougher, and both carry your voice — and a podmate you can talk to but not video-call gets that ceiling named instead of a silently missing button.
  • Each phone's list is its own: the pod list now says reach is per-phone — two podmates can see different lists and both are right, because messages hop phone to phone.
  • Video's honest limit, measured: with no Wi-Fi anywhere, a video call rings, tries, and says it couldn't get through — the video stream needs a shared Wi-Fi (a hotspot with no internet counts), and every surface now says so instead of implying the walkie's no-network trick covers video too. Live voice and voice notes are the no-network tools; carrying video over the direct phone-to-phone link is the next frontier.
  • iPhone: the talk button comes back: a video call could leave the iPhone's audio engine dead in a way it never noticed — press-to-talk read "no audio" and incoming voice played nothing while the who's-talking chip showed. The engine now rebuilds itself the moment it's found dead. And Settings grew a microphone check: one tap tries every way the app can listen and reports which of them works, so a mic bug gets diagnosed from a screenshot instead of a guess.

0.8.2 · The pod gets a face

Aug 25 · the walkie stops needing a network

  • Live voice without Wi-Fi: the walkie used to need a shared network, which is the thing you most often don't have. Two phones now find their own way to each other, and where they can't, the voice still carries over Bluetooth alone — rougher, and marked lo-fi on the row so you know why. You never pick a radio: the app climbs to the best link it can prove and drops back the moment that link goes quiet, and you stay in the pod either way. It's much louder too — the voice comes out of the loudspeaker now, not the earpiece.
  • 1:1 video calls: a 📹 beside each person in the walkie — ring, answer or decline, hang up from either side, mute, flip the camera. It runs over the pod's own link with no server and no internet, and the picture climbs on its own to whatever the two phones can carry. Walk out of range and the call ends with a sentence that says so, instead of freezing.
  • Am I connected, and to whom: one line on the pod card, and a tap opens the list — each podmate with what that connection is actually good for (voice · lo-fi voice · messages now · when you pass by · notes keep). It never names a radio, and a quiet podmate reads as a member who is quiet, not as an error.
  • Messages read like messages: newest at the bottom, opening there and following new arrivals — unless you've scrolled up, in which case your place is kept and a New messages chip offers one tap back down.
  • A voice note that can't play says so: a recording whose save failed left a file with sound in it and no index — the right size, and unplayable by anything, so it travelled across camp anyway. Broken takes are now refused as they happen, and one that arrives damaged is labelled honestly instead of handing you silence.
  • Swap cards by pointing a camera: "We're together — swap cards" opens a panel with your code to hold up and Scan theirs to take one. The app can read a code now — one photo, inside the app, no trip out to the camera and back. A scanned pod invite still asks before it joins.
  • Camp lore the Angel can cite: credited summaries of veteran-burner talks, documentaries and camp histories — the Temple, Rangers, art cars, gifting — each pointing you at the original video. And the Angel can no longer quote the guide's own worked example as if it were a real event.
  • 4 GB iPhones stop crashing: the app sizes the model's memory to the phone it's on instead of pinning the whole thing where iOS can't move it.

0.8.1 · The Angel learns its own strength

Aug 25 · cut, then folded into 0.8.2 above

  • Events answer like a schedule, not a search box: naming an event by its exact title answers instantly, every occurrence, cards first. Titles keep their own words ("Morning" is part of a name, not a time filter), and a time in your question is honored.
  • The camp board survives its own history: a phone restored from a backup rejoins as itself instead of duplicating its board, and replies survive any number of reinstalls.
  • Honesty fixes: a failed model import says so instead of going quiet, a beam from the wrong camp says "wrong passphrase", and the Angel never dresses a search hiccup as an authoritative "nothing found".

0.8.0 · Camp Mesh, consolidated

Aug 24 · your pod, off the grid

  • Pods: pick your people, or join by a spoken code, and your phones keep track of each other over Bluetooth — live direction and distance when you're near. An Android shares from your pocket; an iPhone shares while the app is open. One switch shares, one switch stops everything.
  • A pod you can hand over: a QR and a link, not just four digits read aloud — both carry the pod's name and your card, and scanning one asks before it joins. No radio involved.
  • The answering machine: leave the pod a note — typed or spoken — and it hops phone to phone when podmates pass in range. A spare phone plugged in at camp keeps the mailbox. Mail no longer depends on two phones agreeing what time it is.
  • Walkie: hold to talk, live, on the camp Wi-Fi — a hotspot with no internet behind it is enough. Ten people at once; a bigger pod is pointed at voice notes rather than dropping someone.
  • Dark mode: system-matched or pinned, every label in both palettes, kept honest by a build test.
  • The Angel knows your plans: your hearted events and saved pins are searchable now — written on the phone, never off it.
  • Sharing, sorted: an audit of every share button left almost everything where it was, and moved the one door that takes things IN to a section that says what it accepts.

0.6.1 · The map answers back

Aug 20 · "it doesn't feel very interactive" — now it does

  • Tap anywhere to aim: touch a spot and the compass points at it, named by its real address — "Map spot — 4:36 & G". Tap again from the arrow view and walk there.
  • Hold and drag to place exactly: a tap is fast but coarse. Press and hold for a third of a second and the target lifts off your fingertip, so your hand stops hiding the thing you are placing. Drag until the address reads right, then let go — that is how you pin your camp's actual corner instead of near it.
  • Pinch and pan: two fingers zoom to 4x, one drags the city under you, and the map stays inside its frame at every zoom.
  • Tap a pin to pick it: your saved pins, your friends' camps, the Man and the Temple all answer a tap on the map itself — no more hunting the chips below, and no more dropping a rival pin beside your own.
  • Your camp is a waypoint: set your camp address on your card and Home appears on the map without dropping a pin.
  • Fixed: taps landing centimeters off while zoomed; a press held between 0.22s and 0.35s doing nothing at all; the map flickering while zoomed and again every five seconds while standing still; and a long press crashing the app on some phones.
