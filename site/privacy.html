Playa Pal privacy policy

Last updated: August 25, 2026 · Published by Simbi Community Development, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

The short version: Playa Pal has no server, no accounts, and no analytics. Everything you do in the app stays on your phone unless you yourself beam it to a campmate.

What the app collects

Nothing is transmitted to us — or to anyone — automatically. There is no sign-up, no telemetry, no advertising, no crash reporting service, and no network calls during normal use. The permissions the app asks for are used entirely on your device:

  • Location — to show where you are on the Black Rock City map and point the compass. Your location is used on-screen, in the moment, and is never stored as a history or sent anywhere. If — and only if — you switch on position sharing for a pod, a rounded-off position goes to the phones in that pod over Bluetooth, signed so that only your pod can produce one. One switch in Settings stops all of it.
  • Camera / photos — three uses, all local. To attach a small thumbnail to an art note you write (the full-resolution photo stays in your gallery; only the thumbnail you chose is kept). To read a code: you take one photo, the app decodes it on the phone, and the photo is not kept or sent. And for a video call you place, where the camera is live between two phones and is never recorded.
  • Microphone — only while you are holding a button or on a call. Hold-to-talk on the walkie carries your voice live to the pod and records nothing. A voice note records only while the mic button is held, and then sits on your phone until it travels to your podmates. A video call uses the mic for the length of the call, and is not recorded. Nothing listens in the background, ever.
  • Bluetooth and nearby devices — for the pod: presence, mail, and (when there is no Wi-Fi) live voice, all directly between the phones in your pod. While the app is open and you are in a pod, your phone says "someone in this pod is nearby, ask me for messages" — that signal carries no position, and it is what lets pod messages and voice notes arrive without anyone switching anything on. A rounded-off position goes out only while you have position sharing on, as above. Close the app and all of it stops.
  • Local Wi-Fi — for the walkie and video calls, either over a network you are both on or over a direct link the two phones form themselves. Neither path uses the internet, and calls deliberately have no relay server configured, so there is nothing for a call to route through.

What is kept on your phone

Your conversations with the Angel are stored locally so you can scroll back — the questions, the answers, and what the app looked up to answer them. Older records are pruned automatically. You can export a conversation yourself from Settings; treat that export as private, because it contains the whole conversation. Nothing in it is sent anywhere by the app.

What leaves your phone, and only by your hand

Playa Pal's camp features are phone-to-phone. When you tap "Beam the board" or share a note, the app creates a file (or a QR code) on your screen, and you choose how it travels — AirDrop, Quick Share, a messaging app, a cable. The recipient is whoever you hand it to. Pod messages, voice notes, live voice and video calls travel the same way in spirit: directly between the phones in your pod, with no copy passing through any server of ours, because there is none it could.

Two honest limits, stated rather than implied. A pod code is four digits read aloud, not a password: someone who captures a beacon can work it out and read that pod. And a camp beam is sealed against tampering with your camp's shared passphrase, but it is not encrypted — anyone with the passphrase can read the board. Share accordingly.

Downloads you start

Two things use the internet, both started by you: downloading an AI model (fetched from the model's public host) and any data packs you choose to fetch. These are downloads to your phone, not uploads of anything about you.

Children

Playa Pal is a general-audience navigation and reference app and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included — it does not collect personal information at all.

Changes

If a future version ever changes any of the above, this page and the app's changelog will say so plainly before it ships.
